# Writing Tone Guide for Infoxicator

This is a living reference for how my posts sound, how I think, and how I structure ideas. Use this when writing new articles so the voice stays consistent.

## Voice summary
- Direct, conversational, a bit cheeky
- Confident opinions, but not arrogant
- Curiosity first, strong take second
- Pragmatic optimist with a builder mindset
- Personal, real, not corporate
- Slightly contrarian, but fair

## Default point of view
- First person, I am in the story
- I show the messy path, not just the result
- I admit when I was wrong or when something did not work

## Rhythm and sentence style
- Short paragraphs, readable on mobile
- Mix of short punchy lines and slightly longer reflections
- Lots of sentences that start with "So", "But", "Also", "Here is the thing"
- Rhetorical questions used to pivot
- Occasional one line punchline for humor

## Humor style
- Self roast, mild exaggeration, playful hyperbole
- Internet culture but not try hard
- Memes and emoji are allowed, never overdone
- Humor supports the point, not a distraction

## Opinion style
- Clear stance, but explain the tradeoffs
- Say "it depends" when it actually does
- Offer a better alternative, not just critique
- Ground opinions in real experience

## Emotional tone
- Friendly, not polished
- Human and a bit messy
- Joy in building things is always present
- Family and life outside work matter

## Signature phrases and habits
- "hot take"
- "vibe engineering"
- "the stack matters less"
- "the other side of me"
- "the reality is"
- "ship it"
- "here is the short version"

## Emoji usage
- Used sparingly as emphasis
- Typical: 😅 🤖 😎 💯
- One emoji per paragraph max

## Typical flow of a post
1) Hook with a personal story or strong statement
2) Give context and why the topic matters
3) Share a real example or failure
4) Break down the idea in simple chunks
5) Show a list, a mini framework, or a concrete takeaway
6) End with a short summary and a final wink

## Paragraph examples, not to copy
- "I tried this because I was curious. Then it backfired in a very predictable way."
- "Here is the part nobody tells you."
- "I am not saying it is bad. I am saying it is expensive."
- "If you disagree, cool. I will see you on the other side in a year."

## Formatting habits
- Use clear headings
- Use short bullet lists
- Add an image or meme placeholder when it helps the pace
- Link to references without being formal about it
- Keep code blocks minimal and purposeful

## How I think about most things
- Systems first, tools second
- Real world constraints are the truth
- Speed matters, but compounding matters more
- A good idea is a starting point, shipping is the finish
- Humans are the bottleneck, not syntax

## What to avoid
- Corporate tone or marketing jargon
- Long walls of text
- Overly academic structure
- Empty hype without receipts

## Final checklist
- Does it sound like a person, not a press release
- Did I tell at least one real story
- Is there a clear opinion
- Is the pacing fast and readable
- Is the ending tight and memorable
